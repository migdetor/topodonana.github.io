## Origins

The idea for this map was not premeditated; it simply _happened_. My [PhD thesis](/) has focused on studying the pond network of Doñana National Park: a network of more than 3,300 waterbodies ([Gómez-Rodríguez et al. 2011](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/236586598_Cartografia_de_lagunas_temporales_del_Parque_Nacional_de_Donana), [Díaz-Paniagua et al. 2015](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/305303117_El_Sistema_de_Lagunas_Temporales_de_Donana_una_red_de_habitats_acuaticos_singulares)) with such density, diversity, and connectivity that it provides habitat for more than 600 animal and plant species—many of them rare and/or threatened ([Díaz-Paniagua et al. 2015](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/305303117_El_Sistema_de_Lagunas_Temporales_de_Donana_una_red_de_habitats_acuaticos_singulares); [Green et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-94-007-6173-5_139-1)). These ponds lie on the aeolian mantle—the sands—and most of them are Mediterranean Temporary Ponds ([priority habitat in the EU](https://eunis.eea.europa.eu/habitats/10069); [Zacharias & Zamparas 2010](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10531-010-9933-7)), with their flooding depending on the good status of the aquifer on which they occur ([Custodio et al. 2009](https://www.juntadeandalucia.es/medioambiente/web/Bloques_Tematicos/agencia_andaluza_agua/participacion/publicaciones/libro_donana_baja.pdf)). However, in recent years we have witnessed—and quantified—the degradation of these aquatic ecosystems at an unprecedented pace.

<figure style="margin: 0 0 1rem 0;">
  <img src="{{ "/assets/img/zahillo_red.jpeg" | relative_url }}" alt="Topo Doñana" style="max-width: 100%; height: auto;">
  <figcaption style="font-size: 0.78em; opacity: 0.85; margin-top: 0.35rem; text-align: justify; text-justify: inter-word;">
Comparison of the state of Zahillo pond—one of the most important peridunal ponds in Doñana—in spring 2011 and 2022. In wet years this pond could behave as a permanent one. Photograph: Carmen Díaz-Paniagua.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

In 2018, 83% of the ponds were affected by groundwater abstractions for agriculture and water supply to tourist urbanizations ([de Felipe et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scitotenv.2023.163329)). To the point that 59% of the ponds had disappeared completely ([de Felipe et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scitotenv.2023.163329)), with their basins being colonized by terrestrial vegetation ([Díaz-Paniagua et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1002/aqc.3984); [Díaz-Paniagua et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1163/15685381-bja10179)). The disappearance of ponds has been followed by the loss of their associated biodiversity: aquatic plants ([García-Murillo et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jnc.2024.126814)), invertebrates (Florencio et al. in prep.), amphibians ([Díaz-Paniagua et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1163/15685381-bja10179)) and freshwater turtles ([de Felipe et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biocon.2026.111730)). By 2024, the last permanent pond—_Santa Olalla_—[dried up for the third consecutive year]([link.al.csic](https://www.csic.es/es/actualidad-del-csic/la-laguna-de-santa-olalla-en-donana-se-seca-por-tercer-ano-consecutivo)), marking an unprecedented milestone in Doñana.

For all these reasons, it seemed essential to highlight the abundance and diversity of Doñana’s ponds. Yet when I tried to describe and represent them, I realized that the ponds were barely visible on a 10×5 cm map—the kind that typically accompanies PhD dissertations. I then thought about representing them in A1 format: a physical map on which they could be seen in detail for the first and, perhaps, for many of them, for one last time.

## Background

The idea of producing a map of Doñana is not new:

In **1958**, **J. A. Valverde** published in _British Birds_ ["An ecological sketch of Coto Doñana"](https://britishbirds.co.uk/journal/article/ecological-sketch-coto-donana). For the first time, Doñana was described on the basis of ecological units, their structure, and their functioning. This way of understanding the landscape would culminate in [_Estructura de una comunidad mediterránea de vertebrados terrestres_]([addlink](https://files01.core.ac.uk/download/pdf/36194286.pdf)) and _Vertebrados de las Marismas del Guadalquivir_. Ponds already appear here as an ecological unit.

<figure style="margin: 0 0 1rem 0;">
  <img src="{{ "/assets/img/mosaicsketch.png" | relative_url }}" alt="Topo Doñana" style="max-width: 100%; height: auto;">
  <figcaption style="font-size: 0.78em; opacity: 0.85; margin-top: 0.35rem; text-align: justify; text-justify: inter-word;">
Upper figure: main section of the Coto de Doñana from the Atlantic Ocean, across dunes, pinewoods, and other sandy substrates, to the clays of the marsh. The relative positions of the more detailed sketches of each main zone—shown in the lower part of the figure—are indicated. The vertical axis is not to scale. Lower left: sketch of the surroundings of a pond. Ponds are considered remnants of ancient branches of the Guadalquivir River and are arranged in series along the line separating pinewoods and jaguarzal scrub, so that each pond has pines on one side and *monte blanco* on the other. Lower right: sketch of the cork-oak parkland and the *vera*. The “park” effect is created by the high abundance of cork oaks (Quercus suber) on the jaguarzo plain bordering the marsh—resulting from the moister substrate in this zone. The sketch illustrates how wet depressions are dominated by brambles (Rubus), ferns (Pteridium), and heath (Erica arborea). Extracted from Valverde (1958).
  </figcaption>
</figure>

In **1974**, **C. Allier et al.** produced the first ecological map of the Doñana Biological Reserve. Eleven environmental complexes were distinguished, and permanent ponds, long-hydroperiod ponds, *navazos*, and wet depressions with hydrophytic vegetation were represented.

<figure style="margin: 0 0 1rem 0;">
  <img src="{{ "/assets/img/allier2.jpg" | relative_url }}" alt="Topo Doñana" style="max-width: 100%; height: auto;">
  <figcaption style="font-size: 0.78em; opacity: 0.85; margin-top: 0.35rem; text-align: justify; text-justify: inter-word;">
Detail of the ecological map of the Doñana Biological Reserve at 1:10,000 scale produced by C. Allier, F. González-Bernáldez, and L. Ramírez-Díaz.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

In **1993**, **J. Castroviejo** produced the _Map of Doñana National Park_. The accompanying report compiled, for the first time, the historical, human, climatic, edaphic, and hydrological aspects governing the functioning of Doñana’s different ecosystems, as well as the threats and pressures on the integrity of the National Park. It also included, for the first time, an extensive toponymy, as well as ~70 ponds.

  <figcaption style="font-size: 0.78em; opacity: 0.85; margin-top: 0.35rem; text-align: justify; text-justify: inter-word;">
Detail of the 1:50,000 scale map of Doñana National Park produced by J. Castroviejo.  
  </figcaption>
</figure>

In **1998**, **[C. Montes et al.](https://www.ign.es/web/catalogo-cartoteca/resources/html/023568.html)** produced the [first hierarchical classification of the National Park’s ecosystems](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Francisco-Barrera-6/publication/260182698_Reconocimiento_Biofisico_de_Espacios_Naturales_Protegidos_Donana_una_aproximacion_ecosistenica/links/0c96052fe687226991000000/Reconocimiento-Biofisico-de-Espacios-Naturales-Protegidos-Donana-una-aproximacion-ecosistenica.pdf) through photointerpretation of aerial photographs, satellite imagery, and field verification.

<figure style="margin: 0 0 1rem 0;">
  <img src="{{ "/assets/img/montes.jpg" | relative_url }}" alt="Topo Doñana" style="max-width: 100%; height: auto;">
  <figcaption style="font-size: 0.78em; opacity: 0.85; margin-top: 0.35rem; text-align: justify; text-justify: inter-word;">
Ecological map of Doñana at 1:40,000 scale produced by C. Montes et al.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

In **2005**, **D. Ruiz-Labourdette et al.** published, in their [Map of the marshland complex of the Doñana sand mantles](https://portalrediam.cica.es/geonetwork/static/api/records/0d07732a-71a9-4642-8e79-a99c7cfe5668), the first detailed cartography of marshland formations across the aeolian sand mantles and the coastal spit of El Abalario–Doñana. Using satellite imagery, ~680 ponds distributed between the National and Natural Parks were mapped.

<figure style="margin: 0 0 1rem 0;">
  <img src="{{ "/assets/img/coleto.png" | relative_url }}" alt="Topo Doñana" style="max-width: 100%; height: auto;">
  <figcaption style="font-size: 0.78em; opacity: 0.85; margin-top: 0.35rem; text-align: justify; text-justify: inter-word;">
Map of marshland formations in the Doñana Natural Area at 1:60,000 scale produced by D. Ruiz-Labourdette et al.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

Each of these works has a unique foundational importance, integrating historical, ecological, and human dimensions. However, it was not until the publication of the cartography of temporary ponds in Doñana National Park ([Gómez-Rodríguez et al. 2011](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/236586598_Cartografia_de_lagunas_temporales_del_Parque_Nacional_de_Donana)) that we realized the abundance and importance of Doñana’s ponds had historically been underestimated: a unique ecosystem, yet so threatened that it risks disappearing before many people even become aware of its existence.
